Every evening the keeper of the lighthouse climbs one hundred and twelve steps. She counts them out loud, softly, the way her grandmother taught her, so the tower always knows someone is coming.

At the top she trims the wick and polishes the glass until the whole bay is caught inside it. Far below, the fishing boats turn for home, one small light at a time.

Some nights the fog rolls in so thick that the beam can barely reach the rocks. On those nights she reads aloud instead, letters and old stories, so the sailors can follow her voice to the harbour.
